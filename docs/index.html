UN Court Rules Myanmar Committed Genocide Against Rohingya Minority

C1 · 3 min

In an unprecedented decision, the International Court of Justice has found Myanmar guilty of committing genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority. The verdict, announced by court president Yuji Iwasawa, marks the first time in history that an international tribunal has declared a state responsible for genocide under the 1948 Genocide Convention. The court stated that the military acted with the clear intent to destroy a substantial portion of the population.

The legal battle began seven years ago when The Gambia brought the case to the court in the Netherlands. It focused on large-scale military actions initiated in 2017 in Rakhine State, following attacks by armed Rohingya fighters on police posts. The judges concluded that the subsequent military operations involved deliberate, indiscriminate violence against civilians, including mass killings and widespread sexual abuse, driving over 700,000 Rohingya to seek refuge in neighboring Bangladesh.

While Myanmar officials previously denied the allegations by arguing that their troops were conducting legitimate counter-terrorism operations, the court unanimously rejected those claims. The judges found that genocidal intent was the only reasonable inference to draw from the evidence. Furthermore, the court noted that state institutions engaged in systematic destruction of evidence, including the burning of bodies and villages, while promoting hate speech that dehumanized the minority group.

The ruling leaves a lasting stain on the state, distinguishing this case from previous international trials that targeted individual perpetrators rather than whole governments. Although the court lacks direct power to imprison military officials, it declared that Myanmar has an obligation to pay proportionate financial compensation, with the exact monetary figures to be determined during future legal proceedings.

Human rights groups and Rohingya communities across the globe celebrated the decision as a critical step toward justice after decades of suffering. However, international observers emphasize that the judgment must lead to concrete political action to secure the long-term preservation of the Rohingya people's rights and safety.

Famous Hippo Fiona Dies at Cincinnati Zoo After Swallowing Hat

B1+ · 1 min

Fiona, the beloved hippopotamus who captured global attention following her premature birth in 2017, has died at the Cincinnati Zoo in Ohio. Zoo officials confirmed the tragic news after veterinary teams spent days trying to help her overcome a severe medical emergency.

The incident began approximately ten days ago when a visitor's knitted hat was accidentally thrown into the air, caught by a ceiling fan, and landed inside Fiona's enclosure. The hippo ingested the object, which eventually caused a dangerous obstruction in her small intestine.

Despite emergency medical interventions and constant care, veterinary staff could not save her, as surgical procedures are not feasible for full-grown hippos. Zoo directors expressed profound shock over the outcome, praising the care team for trying everything possible to save the famous animal.

Rohingya Refugees Celebrate Historic Ruling from International Court

B1+ · 1 min

Rohingya refugees living in Bangladesh camps reacted with immense joy and emotional celebrations after learning that the International Court of Justice ruled in their favor. Crowds gathered around public viewing screens to watch the final verdict broadcast live from the Netherlands.

Community members expressed deep relief, calling the decision a long-awaited formal recognition of their immense hardship. For many survivors who fled violent operations in 2017, the court ruling provides renewed hope for future generations seeking safety and legal rights.

Human rights advocate groups emphasized that while the court verdict represents a major legal victory, global leaders must now take action to enforce the ruling and protect displaced communities.

NBTC Proposes Looser Network Coverage Rules to Lower TV Costs

B1+ · 1 min

A recent study by the National Broadcasting and Telecommunications Commission recommends relaxing network coverage requirements for digital television multiplexers, known locally as MUX. The proposal suggests letting operators choose between 80 percent and 95 percent population coverage instead of the current mandatory 95 percent threshold.

Lowering the coverage requirement could significantly reduce monthly rental charges for television channels, providing relief to broadcasters struggling with rising business expenses. Officials noted that the existing rules were designed when terrestrial broadcasting was the main way to deliver television content, which no longer matches modern media habits.

However, regulators must carefully balance cost reductions for businesses with public service obligations. Lowering network reach could impact access to news in remote areas, so the commission must curb unnecessary fees while preserving essential information access for vulnerable communities.

Three Candidates Shortlisted for Chief Executive Role at THAI Airways

B1+ · 1 min

Thai Airways International has narrowed its search for a new chief executive officer, locally called the DD, down to three internal candidates. The selection process comes as the national carrier works to strengthen commercial recovery and maintain operational efficiency following recent financial restructuring.

The finalists include former commercial chief Korakot Chatasingha, technical operations chief Cherdchoke Lathikol, and aviation business head Varangkana Lueroojwong. All three executives bring decades of institutional knowledge, having built long careers across engineering, catering, cargo, and marketing departments within the company.

An overseas human resources consultancy is conducting further assessments before the board makes a final decision in December. The chosen candidate will take office on January 1, 2027, to lead the airline through its next phase of global growth.

Mastering Street Food Travel: Eat Like a Local with Confidence

B2 · 2 min

Exploring street food stalls is often the highlight of international travel, offering an authentic taste of local culture and traditions. However, dining in unfamiliar outdoor markets can feel intimidating for first-time visitors who worry about ordering or health risks. With a thoughtful approach, you can step out of your comfort zone and enjoy delicious regional meals safely.

Before sitting down at a stall, take a moment to observe the customer traffic and overall hygiene. High customer turn-over is one of the best indicators of fresh ingredients, as food is cooked rapidly at high temperatures rather than sitting out for hours. Watching how vendors handle raw meats and cash can also help you make a quick assessment of their food safety practices.

Communicating your preferences across language barriers does not need to be stressful. Simple hand gestures, pointing directly to freshly displayed ingredients, or carrying written translations of your dietary needs can facilitate smooth interactions with vendors. Learning basic local greetings shows respect and often earns warm hospitality from stall owners.

To avoid digestive issues, start small by sampling modest portions before trying complex local dishes. Carrying hand sanitizer and choosing cooked items over raw garnishes can further relieve common travel worries. Embracing street dining allows travelers to support small family vendors while discovering extraordinary flavors that standard hotel restaurants rarely capture.

Joining an Ongoing Conversation

B2 · 2 min

You arrive slightly late to a company dinner reception where several colleagues are already engaged in an animated group discussion. You want to step into the circle gracefully, show genuine curiosity about their topic, and establish a cordial rapport without interrupting anyone mid-sentence.

Thai Speaker's Trap

Direct translation from Thai

Direct Translation: 'Play Internet' vs. 'Use the Internet'

After finishing dinner yesterday, I stayed in my bedroom and played internet until midnight.

After finishing dinner yesterday, I stayed in my bedroom and used the internet until midnight.

Thai speakers often transfer เล่นอินเทอร์เน็ต directly into English as 'play internet' because the verb เล่น commonly introduces leisure activities in Thai. However, native English speakers make a distinct separation between gaming and browsing, using verbs like 'use', 'browse', or 'surf' instead of 'play'. To sound natural in everyday small talk, say you were 'using the internet', 'surfing the web', or simply 'online'.

Error Identification

  1. The court decided that the Myanmar army committed severe human rights violations. — Missing definite article 'the' before specific institution 'Myanmar army'.
  2. Visitors were shocked when the zoo announced that Fiona died from swallowing plastic. — Incorrect verb form after preposition 'from'; gerund 'swallowing' is required.
  3. Refugees celebrated the court ruling which brought hope to their people. — Incorrect pronoun form; possessive adjective 'their' is required before noun.
  4. The commission proposed a new plan to lower regional broadcasting costs. — Incorrect preposition; 'to lower' expresses purpose better than 'for lower'.
  5. The shortlisted candidate discussed her executive background with the board. — Unnecessary preposition 'about' after the transitive verb 'discussed'.
