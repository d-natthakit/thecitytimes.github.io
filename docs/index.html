Cover Story

US Supreme Court Rejects Request to Block California Mail Ballots

C1 · 4 min

The United States Supreme Court on Monday rejected an emergency request from the presidential administration to force California election officials to set aside thousands of mail-in ballots. Federal lawyers had urged the high court to order state authorities to separate approximately 20,000 votes cast by individuals flagged as potential noncitizens on state voter rolls, just hours before the nationwide midterm elections. The ruling represents the latest judicial rebuke regarding federal efforts to intervene in state voting processes.

The emergency appeal was the latest development in a legal battle between federal authorities and California, a Democratic stronghold and the nation's most populous state. Voting in California has been underway since early October, with mail-in ballots distributed to all of the state's roughly 23 million registered voters and hundreds of thousands already processed. The Department of Justice filed its emergency petition with the high court on Monday, one day before midterm elections expected to determine the balance of power in Congress.

Federal prosecutors argued that setting aside challenged ballots was necessary until voters' citizenship status could be verified. The Department of Justice claimed that once disputed ballots were integrated into the main count, identifying and removing noncitizen votes would be impossible. However, California election officials vigorously opposed the request, warning that the federal list compiled by the Department of Homeland Security was unreliable and filled with errors. State leaders cautioned that acceding to the federal request would effectively disenfranchise thousands of eligible legal voters across California.

California authorities emphasized that attempting to locate and isolate specific ballots after weeks of voting would create an unmanageable bureaucratic nightmare for local offices. Because state laws ensure broad voting access through mailed ballots, processing operations were already far advanced when the Justice Department filed its challenge. State election officials maintained that disrupting established procedures on the eve of the election would threaten the overall vote counting process.

In denying the petition, the Supreme Court did not detail its reasoning, which is common for emergency docket decisions. The high court's order follows a decision by Federal District Judge David Carter, who rejected the administration's claims due to concerns regarding database reliability. Judge Carter wrote that if the executive branch could discount votes based on unproven allegations, democracy would be in danger. The justices previously rejected similar attempts to restrict mail ballots in September.

The ruling marks another judicial setback for the administration, which has engaged in unprecedented attempts to influence election management across multiple states. California leaders celebrated the high court's refusal to step in, though election officials remain braced for new attempts to meddle during ballot counting. State officials reiterated warnings that interfering with voters or tampering with ballots violates state law, emphasizing that state government holds primary constitutional responsibility for conducting elections safely and fairly.

World in Brief

Russian Air Strikes Kill 26 at Ukrainian Aid Centre

B1+ · 1 min

A series of Russian air strikes has hit a humanitarian aid distribution centre in southern Ukraine, killing at least 26 people. The attack targeted a local school in the village of Komyshuvakha, located in the Zaporizhzhia region, which local residents used as a weekly distribution site for essential supplies.

According to Ukrainian officials, nine heavy aerial bombs hit the location, damaging the building and causing collapse while emergency workers and civilians were inside. Rescue crews had to scavenge through piles of heavy rubble to retrieve survivors, with initial death tolls rising as more bodies were recovered. Two rescue workers and four police officers were among the casualties reported.

Ukrainian leaders condemned the bombing, stating that military forces were fully aware of the civilian function of the facility. The strike comes amid escalating tension across the region, casting doubt over potential short-term peace talks.

US Plans to Remove South Africa and Rwanda from Trade Programme

B1+ · 1 min

The United States has announced plans to end duty-free trade access for South Africa and Rwanda under the African Growth and Opportunity Act, known as AGOA. US trade officials stated that both nations engaged in practices inconsistent with the program's eligibility standards, citing trade barriers and security concerns.

AGOA has provided qualifying sub-Saharan African nations with tax-free access to the US market for over two decades. South Africa is the largest participant in the program, exporting substantial volumes of vehicle parts, citrus fruits, and wine under its terms.

South African trade representatives indicated they were prepared for the decision, noting that AGOA exports represent a small percentage of total global trade. However, agricultural sectors warned that losing duty-free access could harm local supply chains and threaten regional employment.

Thailand Today

Draft Game Industry Law Facing Major Revisions

B1+ · 1 min

Proposed legislation intended to promote Thailand's gaming sector has returned to the drafting phase following extensive changes by the Digital Economy and Society Ministry. The revisions expand the scope of the original draft beyond digital software to include traditional board games, card games, and competitive electronic sports.

Industry representatives expressed concern over the delays and questioned the proposed governance structure. Under the current revision, public officials would hold a two-thirds majority on the main oversight board, leaving private business leaders with limited influence over policy decisions.

The software association has urged officials to streamline the regulatory framework by removing extra sub-committees and balancing committee seats equally between public and private sectors. Additionally, incorporate a dedicated industry fund into the law adds regulatory steps that require review by financial ministries.

Government Offers Subsidies for Household Solar Systems

B1+ · 1 min

Thailand has launched its Solar for the People scheme, offering grants up to 50,000 baht to help homeowners install residential solar panels. The initiative aims to reduce electricity expenses for up to one million households across the country.

Under the rules, eligible citizens whose names match their property's electricity meter can apply for tailored system packages through state utility providers. While the government will subsidize a portion of the initial equipment, homeowners must cover the remaining installation costs through personal funds or financial loans.

State electricity authorities estimate that a standard installation can lower monthly utility bills significantly. Participating households are also permitted to sell excess power back to the national electrical grid under established rate agreements.

Feature · Tech & Science

Bridging the Digital Divide with Open-Source Tech

B2 · 2 min

Access to modern technology shapes career growth and educational progress around the globe. Yet a broad economic disparity remains between communities with cheap high-speed internet and those lacking basic digital tools. Open-source software and open-hardware designs are emerging as practical solutions to bridge this global divide.

Open-source technology allows engineers and educators everywhere to inspect, modify, and distribute software without paying high license fees. Community-led projects are transforming old computers into functional workstations for schools in developing regions. By using lightweight operating systems, organizations can reinstate aging devices into productive service instead of throwing them away as electronic waste.

Hardware innovation is following a similar path. Low-cost single-board microcontrollers enable local innovators to build customized tools, from low-cost weather stations to automated farming systems. These modular designs undergo open scrutiny from global developer communities, ensuring rapid improvements and lowering production expenses.

Adopting open-source frameworks also encourages self-reliance within local tech sectors. Rather than relying entirely on proprietary systems from foreign tech giants, local developers acquire deep practical skills by modifying code for their immediate environment. This approach builds local technical expertise while keeping operational costs manageable.

To maximize these benefits, non-profit groups and educational institutions must focus on training programs alongside hardware distribution. Teaching basic programming and system administration ensures that communities can maintain their infrastructure independently, turning temporary technical aid into sustainable local development.

Real English · Two Takes

Should tech companies replace human software testers entirely with automated AI testing tools?

C1 · 2 min

Efficiency and Scale

Automated tools can execute thousands of code tests in minutes, finding system bugs far faster than human teams. Granted that initial setup requires effort, automation eliminates human error in repetitive checks. Software teams can release updates continuously without waiting for manual approval cycles.

Human Intuition Matters

Software is designed for humans, not scripts. Automated tools miss creative edge cases and user experience issues that a real person notices immediately. It appears that complete automation saves money, but in stark contrast to algorithm checks, human testers understand emotional context and real usability.

Argument Phrases

  • It appears that — hedging
  • in stark contrast to — contrast

Thai Speaker's Trap

Prepositions

Incorrect Preposition After 'Discuss'

We will discuss about the project plan tomorrow.

We will discuss the project plan tomorrow.

In English, the verb 'discuss' takes a direct object without a preposition. Thai speakers often add 'about' because of the direct translation from 'พูดเกี่ยวกับ' (phut kiakoap).

Error Identification

  1. The court rejected a request to block mail ballots to prevent disenfranchising voters. — The verb prevent takes a gerund (-ing form), not an infinitive.
  2. Officials aim to streamline bureaucratic rules so companies can operate efficiently. — Use an adverb (efficiently) to modify the verb operate.
  3. The government will subsidize solar panels to reduce electricity bills. — Use to + infinitive (to reduce) to express purpose, not for + infinitive.
  4. Developers will incorporate security features to protect systems against escalating threats. — The verb protect normally collocates with from or against, but against is preferred for active threats.
  5. Public scrutiny helped uncover a wide disparity in funding between schools. — The noun disparity usually takes the preposition in when referring to a domain or area.
