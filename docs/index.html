France Accuses Iran of Cyberattack on Supermarket Fridges

C1 · 3 min

France has formally accused Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) of conducting a series of major cyber-attacks that disrupted food storage systems across the country last month. According to French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot, hackers deliberately raised the temperatures of fridges and freezers in hundreds of grocery stores, forcing retailers to destroy tons of chilled and frozen goods. Barrot described the incident as an unprecedented act of aggression aimed at national sabotage and vital security interests.

The cyber incident took place on October 11, when attackers targeted specialized software created by the Danish engineering firm Danfoss, which allows store owners to control cold-storage systems remotely. The breach affected over 100 Cooperative U stores as well as 180 branches of Intermarche and Netto. In several locations, overnight storage temperatures spiked to 50 degrees Celsius. Other major national chains, including Carrefour, Monoprix, and E.Leclerc, also reported system disruptions.

In response, the French government summoned the Iranian ambassador to Paris to lodge a formal protest. Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau confirmed that an official investigation has opened against the head and two deputies of Shahid Kaveh, a specialized cyber unit affiliated with the IRGC. Minister Barrot stated that France would take all necessary and proportionate measures to defend its infrastructure and create an effective deterrence against future digital threats.

While Iranian media outlets dismissed the claims as baseless, commentators in Tehran have recently suggested expanding political retaliation beyond the Middle East if regional conflict escalates. Analysts remain uncertain whether the digital attack was intended as a warning signal. The incident was followed by a second wave of hacks in November that affected over 100 stores in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia, and Finland, though official connections between these events remain unconfirmed.

Although two Russian-aligned hacking groups briefly claimed responsibility, international investigators largely dismissed those assertions. Cyber security experts note that technical attribution remains exceptionally difficult because advanced state actors routinely hide their locations. Nevertheless, French authorities insist that their findings clearly point to state-sponsored actors working within Iran's military apparatus.

Nvidia Investors Sue CEO Over Alleged Intelligence Ties

B1+ · 2 min

Shareholders have filed a proposed class-action lawsuit against Nvidia and its chief executive, Jensen Huang, accusing them of failing to disclose potential national security risks. The legal filing in California claims the executive hid a relationship with Alice Huang, a former foreign corporate executive identified by US authorities as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset.

The legal challenge follows reports of an ongoing FBI investigation into foreign intelligence targets within American technology firms. In an open letter, Huang admitted to an extramarital affair without naming the individual, while promising full cooperation with an independent corporate inquiry. President Donald Trump expressed personal support for the chief executive, noting that Huang denied knowing about any espionage activity.

China's Foreign Ministry strongly rejected the claims, labeling the espionage allegations as completely unsubstantiated smears. Meanwhile, Nvidia's share price fell by 4.3% following the legal disclosures. The case has raised sharp concerns among investors about corporate leadership and security protocols at the world's leading artificial intelligence chipmaker.

British Comedian Russell Brand Sentenced for Sexual Assaults

B1+ · 1 min

Former British television host and comedian Russell Brand has been sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison after being convicted of rape and sexual assault in a London court. Judge Justice Bennathan handed down the ruling at Southwark Crown Court following a high-profile trial that featured testimony from six anonymous witnesses.

The jury found the 51-year-old guilty on four counts relating to incidents between 2004 and 2009, including the rape of a woman in 2009. Victims delivered emotional impact statements before sentencing, detailing long-term harm. Prosecutors argued that despite his changing public media persona, a clear pattern of misogyny and predatory behavior characterized his career.

Brand continuously denied all allegations, asserting that all his past sexual relationships were fully consensual. His legal team is expected to evaluate appellate options, but the lengthy prison sentence marks a total fall for the former international celebrity.

Luxury Cruise Ship Detained in Singapore Over Unpaid Debts

B1+ · 1 min

The luxury cruise vessel MV Odyssey has been detained in Singapore following a legal filing over unpaid fuel debts. The legal action was initiated by a Singaporean law firm representing creditors after the ship's operator, Florida-based Villa Vie Residences, failed to cover approximately 16.75 million baht in fuel expenses.

The vessel had abruptly ended its planned three-year world voyage in late October at Sihanoukville, Cambodia. Around 200 international passengers were forced to disembark with minimal notice after the operator claimed that soaring global fuel costs made ongoing operations financial unsustainable. Over 90 former passengers remained scattered across Cambodian towns, including Phnom Penh, while arranging alternate travel.

The Odyssey eventually departed Cambodia without passengers on November 4 before being seized by maritime authorities in Singapore. Legal experts note that the court-ordered detention could eventually lead to an auction of the vessel if outstanding operational debts remain unpaid by the owners.

Thai Consumers Show Strong Enthusiasm for AI Tools

B1+ · 1 min

A new survey by market research firm Ipsos reveals that 74% of Thai consumers view artificial intelligence positively, compared to a global average of just 55%. Additionally, 80% of employed Thai respondents stated that digital automation tools actively help them save valuable time during their daily work routines.

Despite high enthusiasm, the study highlighted significant consumer concerns regarding personal data security and commercial transparency. Only 35% of respondents consider automated chatbots trustworthy, while 85% believe businesses must clearly disclose when automation is used. Furthermore, 79% felt corporate customer services were becoming excessively impersonal.

The findings also show a broader trend toward digital reliance across generations, alongside rising anxiety about economic changes. Analysts suggest that companies wanting to succeed in the market must address privacy demands while modernizing their digital offerings.

The Hidden Energy Costs of Our Connected World

B2 · 2 min

Every time we stream a video, query an artificial intelligence model, or save photos to cloud servers, we set off a chain reaction across global digital infrastructure. While cloud services feel invisible and weightless, the physical data centers supporting our daily habits require enormous amounts of electrical power and water cooling to operate continuously.

Recent technology trends have slashed processing times for complex business tasks, but computing demands have grown exponentially. Modern server facilities consume more electricity than many small nations. The physical footprint of the internet includes massive warehouse buildings filled with high-performance computers that operate continuously, generating vast quantities of heat that must be managed.

This expanding infrastructure places unique pressure on regional power grids. In many regions, utility providers struggle to balance residential electricity needs with the immense demands of technological hubs. Engine designers and computer engineers are searching for solution pathways, exploring liquid cooling systems and localized renewable microgrids to reduce operational impacts.

Addressing this challenge does not require individuals to abandon digital tools. Instead, technology sector leaders emphasize the importance of software efficiency and hardware sustainability. Designing lightweight code and optimizing server utilization can significantly lower energy requirements without compromising performance.

Consumers can also play a role by adopting mindful digital habits. Simple actions like cleaning out unused cloud storage, avoiding unnecessary high-definition video background streaming, and using local device processing when possible can reduce collective server loads. Balancing digital convenience with energy awareness remains a key responsibility in our modern age.

Real English · Two Takes

Should working professionals completely disconnect from work communication channels during their annual leave?

C1 · 2 min

Protect True Rest

Employees must disconnect entirely while on vacation. Constant notifications create mental fatigue and prevent true recovery. Although urgent matters occasionally arise, proper delegation prevents workflow breakdowns. Checking messages during leave signals an overreliance on individuals rather than robust systems. While emergency contact protocols could exist, complete detachment remains necessary for long-term health.

Maintain Peace of Mind

A brief daily check of unread messages can prevent overwhelming stress upon return. Staying aware of critical updates allows professionals to handle minor issues early, preventing major workplace crises. Even though total disconnection sounds ideal, completely ignoring inbox updates often causes severe return anxiety. With reasonable boundaries, light monitoring supports professional continuity without ruining rest.

Argument Phrases

  • Although urgent matters occasionally arise — concession
  • complete detachment remains necessary — hedging
  • Even though total disconnection sounds ideal — contrast

Thai Speaker's Trap

Prepositions

Incorrect Preposition After 'Arrive'

We arrived to the office early this morning.

We arrived at the office early this morning.

Thai speakers often use 'to' after 'arrive' because the Thai word 'ถึง' (theung) translates directly as moving toward a destination. In English, use 'arrive at' for specific places like offices or buildings, and 'arrive in' for cities or countries. Never use 'arrive to'.

Error Identification

  1. The government launched an inquiry after cyberattacks caused massive disruption in public services. — Retaliation means revenge against an attacker, which does not fit the context of public service disruption.
  2. The company relies too much on contractor work, leading to an overreliance on temporary staff. — Overreliance takes the preposition 'on', not 'to'.
  3. The court decided that the punishment was fully proportionate to the crime committed. — Proportionate takes the preposition 'to', not 'with'.
  4. The executive denied allegations regarding an extramarital affair during the press conference. — The singular article 'an' requires the singular noun 'affair'.
  5. The store slashed its prices to attract more shoppers during the holiday season. — The possessive pronoun is 'its', whereas 'it's' is a contraction for 'it is'.
