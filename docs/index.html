Patrick Stewart, Celebrated Shakespearean Actor and 'Star Trek' Captain, Dies at 86

C1 · 3 min

Patrick Stewart, the acclaimed British actor whose career bridged classical Shakespearean theatre and celebrated Hollywood science fiction, has died at the age of 86. A longtime spokesperson, Kelly Bush Novak, confirmed that he passed away on Wednesday at his home in Los Angeles following a battle with Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia. Family and close friends surrounded him during his final moments, remembering his invincible grace and emotional warmth.

Stewart grew up in Mirfield, a working-class town in northern England, in a modest home without an indoor bathroom. Despite early hardship and domestic violence from his father, he discovered a passion for acting at age 12. A scholarship to the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School helped launch his formal training. By age 19, he had lost his hair to alopecia, which later helped define his striking physical presence onstage.

Stewart soon established himself as an extraordinarily versatile performer. He completed a 14-year stint with the prestigious Royal Shakespeare Company, taking on demanding roles including Shylock, Oberon, and Claudius. Decades later, his 2007 performance in 'Macbeth' earned him a Tony Award nomination. Decades of classical stage work gave him a commanding baritone voice and deep emotional discipline, making him an anchor of British theatre before international audiences knew his name.

Global stardom arrived in 1987 when Hollywood cast him as Captain Jean-Luc Picard in 'Star Trek: The Next Generation'. Although Stewart initially suspected the syndicated show would fail, it ran for seven seasons and 178 episodes. His measured gravitas, alongside famous commands like 'make it so' and 'engage!', defined the series and inspired four feature films. Decades later, he returned to the iconic role in 'Star Trek: Picard' and reached comic-book fans as Professor Charles Xavier in the popular 'X-Men' film series.

Beyond the stage and screen, Stewart devoted considerable energy to humanitarian causes. Having witnessed his mother suffer physical abuse at home, he became an outspoken patron of Refuge, a charity supporting survivors of domestic violence. He also served as an ambassador for the International Rescue Committee. Queen Elizabeth II knighted him in June 2010 for his exceptional artistic contributions.

Stewart's death marks the loss of an artist who brought classical Shakespearean weight into modern popular cinema. Tributes poured in from around the globe, with colleagues praising his remarkable intellect, humanity, and quiet strength. Both science-fiction admirers and theatre enthusiasts mourn a performer whose artistic standards elevated every role he played.

High-Speed Rail Crash in Central Spain Leaves 69 Dead

B1+ · 1 min

A tragic rail collision in central Spain killed at least 69 people and injured more than 100 on Wednesday morning. The crash occurred near Urda when a high-speed Renfe train struck a stationary Iryo train on the same track, causing multiple carriages to derail.

Authorities confirmed that the victims include 24-year-old long jumper Julieta Gallego and former employment minister Fátima Báñez, who introduced controversial labour reforms in 2012. An officer from the Civil Guard and a finance lawyer were also among those who lost their lives.

Forensic teams in Toledo are collecting DNA samples to identify bodies from the wreckage. Meanwhile, train drivers launched strikes to demand higher safety investments, and officials are investigating the catastrophic collision.

Spain Enters Three Days of Mourning Following Rail Disaster

B1+ · 1 min

Spain began three official days of national mourning on Thursday after Wednesday's catastrophic train collision near Urda. The terrible impact left 50 hospitalized passengers still receiving medical care, while emergency crews used cranes and drones to inspect mangled train carriages.

Transport Minister Óscar Puente called the collision highly unlikely and suggested human error under inexplicable circumstances caused the crash. Notably, this tragedy occurred just 10 months after another deadly collision involving the same two rail operators in Andalucía killed 46 people.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez pledged that state institutions are working day and night to establish the precise cause. Authorities aim to uncover every detail and upgrade safety protocols across the entire high-speed network.

Street Food Smarts: How to Eat Safely and Authentically While Traveling

B2 · 2 min

Exploring a foreign country through its roadside food stalls is one of the greatest pleasures of travel. Street markets offer intimate cultural insights that luxury dining establishments rarely replicate. However, navigating open-air stalls in an unfamiliar city requires a balanced approach to stay healthy.

The most essential rule for travelers is following local crowds. High foot traffic means ingredients turn over rapidly, minimizing the risk of food spoilage under warm weather. Look for vendors who cook each order over fresh heat rather than displaying pre-made dishes at room temperature.

Smart diners also steer clear of tap water and unpeeled raw vegetables in regions where local sanitation varies. Choosing thoroughly boiled soups, piping hot noodles, and freshly grilled meats allows you to enjoy famous regional delicacies safely without worrying about stomach distress later.

Being an adventurous eater does not require throwing caution away. Watch how the vendor handles currency versus raw ingredients. If the person taking payment does not touch the food directly, that is a reliable sign of basic hygiene awareness.

Finally, observe dining etiquette around communal tables. Sharing a bench with neighborhood residents provides meaningful small talk and priceless culinary recommendations. With minor precautions, roadside dining becomes a safe, memorable highlight of your journey.

Thai Speaker's Trap

Direct translation from Thai

Using 'Take Care' Instead of 'Pay Attention'

Please take care of the cooking instructions on the food label.

Please pay attention to the cooking instructions on the food label.

Thai speakers often say 'take care' because the Thai word 'ดูแล' (duu-laae) or 'ระวัง' (ra-wang) is used broadly for both looking after someone and being attentive. In English, you 'take care of' people, pets, or tasks, but you 'pay attention to' instructions, signs, or warnings.
