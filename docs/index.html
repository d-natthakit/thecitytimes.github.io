Cover Story

Tech Outage Hits United Airlines Days After US Flight Shutdown

C1 · 3 min

United Airlines announced on Monday that it had resolved a major system failure that caused significant flight delays across the United States. The Chicago-based airline confirmed that while departing flights experienced disruptions, airborne flights and those operated by its regional partner, United Express, were completely unaffected. The company emphasized that there was no indication that the incident resulted from a cybersecurity breach.

According to internal union documents, the web-based outage disabled critical software applications used by pilots in the cockpit. These digital tools allow flight crews to calculate essential preflight parameters, such as aircraft weight, fuel levels, baggage, and weather conditions, to ensure safe takeoffs. Without functioning software, pilots were instructed to follow local ground guidance while departing flights remained stranded at airport gates.

The operational breakdown affected more than four hundred flights on Monday afternoon, highlighting how vulnerable modern aviation remains to digital technology failures. The event occurred just three days after a massive Federal Aviation Administration network malfunction grounded commercial flights nationwide for over four hours. United Airlines clarified that Monday's operational issues were entirely separate from Friday's system breakdown, which federal officials blamed on routine maintenance work.

This disruption is part of a broader series of recent technological meltdowns across major US industries. On the same day, online banking services at several prominent financial institutions experienced severe access issues after a technical glitch at JPMorgan Chase. Furthermore, a massive software breakdown at Microsoft late last week paralyzed essential communication platforms worldwide, causing serious repercussions for hospitals, businesses, and government agencies.

Aviation experts note that airlines have repeatedly faced operational impediments due to legacy computer infrastructure. In August 2025, United suffered a similar grounding after an internal flight data system failed. As global transport and financial networks face growing public scrutiny, industry leaders face rising pressure to upgrade critical systems and prevent cascading failures.

World in Brief

Five European Nations Plan Joint Deportation Hub in Rwanda

B1+ · 1 min

Five European countries have announced plans to open a joint deportation centre in Rwanda by 2027 to manage illegal migration. Germany, Austria, Denmark, Greece, and the Netherlands agreed to establish the external hub for individuals who have exhausted all legal avenues to remain in Europe.

Under the agreement, deportees who cannot return to their countries of origin will be transferred to Rwanda. Once there, individuals will either integrate into local society or prepare for returning home. The proposal differs from a canceled British scheme, which aimed to process asylum claims abroad.

While participating governments insist the hub complies with international law, human rights groups express strong concern over potential rights violations. Meanwhile, leaders from other European nations have publicly criticized the plan, arguing that offshore deportation hubs lack humanity.

European Football Chief Launches Challenge for FIFA Leadership

B1+ · 1 min

French Football Federation President Philippe Diallo has officially entered the race to challenge Gianni Infantino for the FIFA presidency. European football's governing body, UEFA, voted unanimously to back Diallo ahead of the upcoming election scheduled for March in Morocco.

Diallo announced his candidacy following growing friction between European officials and Infantino over financial proposals and global tournament governance. The French official joins Japanese administrator Kohzo Tashima as formal challengers to the incumbent chief, who has led world football since 2016.

If successful, Diallo would become the first black president in FIFA history. Infantino has faced mounting criticism in recent months over governance decisions and controversies surrounding high-profile political interactions during major international tournaments.

Thailand and US Sign Trade Agreement at ASEAN Summit

B1+ · 1 min

Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul and US President Donald Trump are scheduled to sign a joint statement on reciprocal trade in Manila. The signing marks a significant moment during the final day of the 49th ASEAN Summit, an annual meeting of Southeast Asian leaders.

The bilateral agreement focuses on expanding market access, attracting international investment, and enhancing economic security for both nations. Thai officials confirmed that the government prioritizes the preservation of export opportunities and boosting business competitiveness across regional markets.

The ceremony concludes an intensive series of summit meetings involving regional partners, including Japan, China, India, and South Korea. Following the trade signing, Thailand will participate in the formal handover of the ASEAN chairmanship to Singapore for 2027.

Tourism Authority Targets New European Markets to Reduce Risk

B1+ · 1 min

The Tourism Authority of Thailand, the government agency responsible for promoting travel, is launching new marketing campaigns to diversify its visitor base. The strategy aims to reduce reliance on Middle Eastern flight hubs affected by ongoing regional instability.

Tourism officials are expanding outreach into Eastern Europe and secondary cities in Western Europe. To boost arrivals, the agency is coordinating seasonal charter flights and collaborating with international travel companies to turn consumer interest into direct bookings.

Data indicates rapid growth from markets like Poland, supported by expanded direct flights to Bangkok. Officials believe targeting new regions will secure stable tourist numbers and ensure long-term economic resilience for the domestic hospitality sector.

Feature · Tech & Science

Building Digital Resilience in an Interconnected World

B2 · 2 min

Modern society relies heavily on digital infrastructure to manage daily operations, from public transit systems to international banking. However, recent corporate software failures demonstrate how easily routine activities can collapse when single points of failure break down. When interconnected systems crash simultaneously, organizations quickly realize that digital reliance brings unexpected vulnerabilities alongside productivity gains.

Achieving true technological resilience requires more than basic software updates; it demands comprehensive risk management strategies. Industry leaders must prioritize regular infrastructure audits and implement redundant backup channels for critical workflows. When core communication platforms fail, teams need immediate access to secondary tools to prevent complete operational paralysis across business units.

Human preparation is equally vital when navigating automated system disruptions. Employees who rely exclusively on automated software often struggle when sudden technical glitches force a manual fallback. Organizations that routinely practice emergency procedures ensure that staff members remain capable of managing essential operations manually during unexpected digital blackouts.

Furthermore, technical teams must design software architecture with compartmentalization in mind. Isolating critical modules prevents a localized bug from triggering cascading failures throughout a global network. By creating strict operational boundaries between software components, companies can contain technical errors before they cause widespread commercial damage.

As digital ecosystems grow increasingly complex, brief software interruptions are becoming inevitable. Rather than assuming systems will function perfectly, forward-thinking organizations build flexible operational frameworks. Investing in systemic resilience today protects businesses from costly disruptions and maintains public trust in an unpredictable technological landscape.

Real English · Two Takes

Should software development teams ban automated code generators for junior developers?

C1 · 2 min

Enforce Core Skills

Junior developers must master foundational programming concepts before relying on automated tools. Relying on AI assistants early in a career creates artificial confidence without true technical understanding. While automation speeds up routine coding task output, early career engineers need to solve difficult problems manually to build deep analytical skills. Allowing artificial tools too early prevents new hires from grasping fundamental system mechanics.

Embrace Modern Workflows

Banning modern tools isolates junior engineers from industry realities. Today's software environment demands efficiency, and automated assistants help junior developers learn syntax rapidly while tackling complex projects. Granted that basic knowledge remains essential, using modern assistance lets entry-level staff focus on higher-level system architecture rather than repetitive syntax errors. Restricting access simply hinders professional growth in a fast-paced market.

Argument Phrases

  • While automation speeds up routine coding task output — concession
  • creates artificial confidence without true technical understanding — contrast
  • Granted that basic knowledge remains essential — hedging

Thai Speaker's Trap

Prepositions

Prepositions: 'Depend On' vs. 'Depend With'

Our success depends with our software reliability.

Our success depends on our software reliability.

Thai speakers often translate the concept of depending on something directly using 'with' because of phrases like ขึ้นอยู่กับ (khuen yu kap). In English, the verb 'depend' always takes the preposition 'on' or 'upon'. Never use 'with' after depend when stating a conditional relationship.
