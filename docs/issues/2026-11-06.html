Paraguayan President Santiago Peña Resigns Amid Financial Turmoil

C1 · 3 min

Paraguayan President Santiago Peña has resigned from office following a swift political crisis caused by the collapse of a prominent digital lender, Ueno Bank. The sudden downfall of the financial institution, which held government accounts and served approximately three million customers—representing roughly 60 percent of the nation's adult population—left millions of citizens unable to access their funds and derailed public confidence in the administration.

The crisis escalated rapidly when the Central Bank of Paraguay intervened in Ueno Bank after determining that the lender had breached minimum solvency requirements. The central bank seized control of the institution and initiated an insolvency process, freezing all operations. In response to the frozen accounts, widespread public protests erupted in the streets, with citizens demanding President Peña's immediate resignation.

Lawmakers in Congress quickly moved against the president, accusing him of misconduct, severe negligence, and potential conflicts of interest. Peña had been a shareholder until last year in the parent company that owned the digital bank. Opposition lawmakers called an extraordinary legislative session to launch an impeachment trial, charging Peña with complicity in covering up the institution's insolvency and mismanaging public pension funds that were deposited there.

As Peña lost critical political backing from members of his own conservative Colorado Party, he released a pre-recorded video statement on social media announcing his decision to step down. In his address, Peña denied all allegations of impropriety, stating that he was stepping aside to protect social peace, national stability, and democratic institutions. He apologized to the public for any perceived failures during his tenure.

Following the resignation, Vice President Pedro Alliana is set to assume the presidency. Meanwhile, Congress has approved a measure to process payments for affected depositors, and a new central bank chief has been appointed to manage the emergency. However, demonstrators outside the legislative building continued to protest, urging lawmakers not to formally accept the resignation so that Peña could face a full trial.

California Governor Commutes All Death Sentences to Life Terms

B1+ · 1 min

California Governor Gavin Newsom has announced that he will commute the death sentences of all 548 inmates currently on the state's death row to life imprisonment without the possibility of parole. In a public letter, Newsom stated that state-sanctioned execution is incompatible with democratic values and undermines the justice system.

Under California's state constitution, the governor requires the approval of the state supreme court to grant clemency to individuals convicted of multiple felonies. Consequently, the court must review and approve 382 of the planned commutations before they can take full effect.

The decision has drawn praise from human rights advocates who argue that capital punishment is flawed and racially biased. However, Newsom acknowledged the emotional difficulty of the move for victims' families, noting that voters had previously rejected a ballot measure to abolish the death penalty in 2016.

Melania Trump Hosts First-Ever Peace Meeting with Russian and Ukrainian Envoy

B1+ · 1 min

United States First Lady Melania Trump hosted high-level officials from Russia and Ukraine at the White House on Thursday, encouraging both nations to negotiate a lasting peace. The diplomatic gathering follows a recent six-day localized humanitarian ceasefire in the Oleshky area, which permitted aid distribution and safe evacuations.

During the meeting, Ukrainian representatives presented the First Lady with the Order of Princess Olga, an official honor awarded by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Mrs. Trump also delivered personal letters addressed to the leaders of both nations, emphasizing her role as a diplomatic intermediary established in written agreements.

The temporary truce in Oleshky expired without an extension, and subsequent attacks in other regions caused significant civilian casualties. Nevertheless, international mediators remain hopeful as new diplomatic discussions mediated by the United States are scheduled to take place in Abu Dhabi later this month.

Changan Automobile Unveils Strategic Three-Year Plan in Thailand

B1+ · 1 min

Chinese electric vehicle maker Changan Automobile has announced a comprehensive three-year strategy to expand its manufacturing presence in Thailand from 2027 to 2029. The company aims to introduce 12 new vehicle models and sell 30,000 units in 2026, supporting the government policy targeting zero-emission cars.

As part of its operations, Changan plans to foster local supply chains by raising the percentage of Thai-made components in its vehicles from 50 percent to over 60 percent. The automaker will also expand its supplier network to over 400 local partners, providing components for domestic production and global exports.

The company's expansion includes extending its service network to more than 150 locations across the country and hiring over 3,000 workers by 2030. Additionally, Changan plans to export battery electric vehicles produced at its Rayong facility to 18 nations as part of its global growth initiative.

Amnesty Urges Thailand Not to Deport Uyghur Detainees to China

B1+ · 1 min

Human rights organization Amnesty International has called on Thai authorities to halt the planned deportation of two Uyghur men to China. The group urged the government to allow the men access to legal counsel and help them seek sanctuary in a safe third country to protect their fundamental rights.

The two individuals were released from prison under a royal pardon—an official act of clemency granted by the monarch—and subsequently transferred to an immigration detention center in Bangkok. They are among five remaining Uyghurs in Thailand from a larger group detained in 2014 after fleeing persecution in Xinjiang.

Amnesty warned that returning the men would violate international non-refoulement obligations, which prohibit transferring individuals to places where they face a grave risk of torture or detention. The organization stressed that assurances from foreign governments cannot override international legal protections.

Feature · Food & Travel

Navigating Group Dining Across Cultures with Confidence

B2 · 2 min

Traveling overseas or joining international colleagues for a shared meal is one of life's great pleasures. Food brings people together, but dining traditions vary significantly around the world. Understanding common dining rules can help you navigate social gatherings smoothly, avoid awkward moments, and show genuine respect for your host's culture.

Different cultures maintain unique expectations around table manners and sharing food. In many Western countries, diners usually order individual meals rather than sharing dishes, and sticking to proper Western dining etiquette is appreciated. Conversely, family-style sharing is standard across much of Asia. When participating in shared meals, using designated serving utensils rather than personal cutlery helps maintain cleanliness and shows consideration for others.

Accepting food graciously is another key aspect of social dining. While hosts love seeing guests enjoy regional delicacies, dietary restrictions or personal preferences might compel you to decline certain items. When turning down a dish, polite refusal combined with warm appreciation for the meal prevents any misunderstanding. A simple compliment about the overall spread can mitigate any potential discomfort.

Paying the bill can also create awkwardness if expectations are unclear. In some cultures, one person hosting the gathering insists on paying for everyone, while in others, splitting the check equally is standard practice. Observing your companions or quietly asking the organizer beforehand helps establish a clear consensus without creating unnecessary tension at the table.

Ultimately, thoughtful guests focus on enjoying the conversation and welcoming hospitality. A versatile traveler approaches new culinary customs with flexibility and polite curiosity. By observing basic cultural norms and communicating clearly, you can make every shared meal an enjoyable experience for everyone involved.

Thai Speaker's Trap

Direct translation from Thai

Direct Translation: 'Eat Alcohol'

We are going to eat alcohol at the party tonight.

We are going to drink alcohol at the party tonight.

In Thai, people often use the word กิน (kin) for both eating food and drinking beverages, leading to phrases like 'กินเหล้า'. In English, however, you must use 'drink' for liquids and alcoholic beverages. Using 'eat' with liquids sounds unnatural to native speakers.
