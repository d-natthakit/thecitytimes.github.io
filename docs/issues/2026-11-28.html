Canada Supreme Court Largely Upholds Quebec Ban on Religious Symbols

C1 · 3 min

Canada's top court has largely upheld a secular law in Quebec that bars public sector employees from wearing visible religious symbols at work. The ruling confirms that the province acted within its constitutional authority when enacting Bill 21, a contentious statute that prohibits teachers, police officers, government lawyers, and judges from wearing items such as hijabs, turbans, crosses, and kippahs while performing their duties.

The legal battle began when Quebec passed the measure in 2019 by invoking the notwithstanding clause, a constitutional mechanism that allows provincial legislatures to override certain protected liberties temporarily. Various groups, including Muslim, Sikh, and Catholic educators, initiated legal challenges, arguing that the legislation deeply harmed their basic freedoms. The top court determined that Quebec could shield the statute from most court challenges, though it noted that judges still hold the authority to assess whether provincial measures match the national charter.

The judgment was not entirely unanimous in every aspect. Writing for the majority, Chief Justice Richard Wagner observed that the legislation reflects the province's historical commitment to secular governance. However, the court established one limit by ruling that the ban cannot prevent elected officials from wearing face coverings, finding that such an exclusion infringes upon fundamental democratic rights. Three dissenting justices also warned that the override clause should never validate policies incompatible with a free society.

Civil liberties advocates swiftly condemned the decision. Stephen Brown, chief executive of the National Council of Canadian Muslims, argued that the policy effectively bars entire categories of people from public sector careers simply because of their faith. Representatives from the English Montreal School Board conveyed strong disappointment, though they acknowledged that certain judicial declarations offered helpful clarity. Meanwhile, Quebec officials defended the policy as a vital effort to uphold state neutrality, noting that recent initiatives have extended similar secular rules across daycares and post-secondary institutions.

The outcome concludes one of the most extensive hearings in the supreme court's history, which involved fifty-one participating interveners and six provincial attorneys general. Prime Minister Mark Carney indicated that federal officials will evaluate the legal implications, emphasizing that all governments must respect minority rights. The decision solidifies Quebec's distinctive legal framework while leaving significant debate nationwide regarding how Canadian institutions balance collective regional identities against individual religious freedoms.

US Disables Commercial Ship in Gulf of Oman Under Blockade

B1+ · 1 min

The United States military has fired precision munitions at a commercial vessel in the Gulf of Oman after it reportedly ignored warnings to halt. According to US Central Command, the Palau-flagged Pacific Archer had departed the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas before forces acted to deploy defensive strikes against its propulsion system.

American defense officials stated that the military action left the civilian crew unharmed while disabling the vessel's engines. Washington established a naval blockade of Iranian ports in mid-July after diplomatic arrangements collapsed, following earlier hostilities that began in February.

The operation brings the total number of ships disabled under the blockade to six, with more than 150 others redirected. Meanwhile, regional tensions increased further as Kuwait summoned Iran's ambassador after a drone strike killed two Italian technicians at a military facility.

OpenAI Sues Apple Over Trade Secrets and Talent Retention

B1+ · 1 min

OpenAI has filed a federal lawsuit against Apple in California, accusing the tech giant of stealing trade secrets and discouraging hardware suppliers from collaborating on future devices. The counterclaims follow an earlier lawsuit by Apple alleging that OpenAI improperly poached engineers to obtain proprietary design information.

In its court filing, OpenAI claimed Apple gained confidential data regarding product roadmaps by rehiring an employee who had worked at OpenAI for six months. Apple denied the claims, calling them an attempt to deflect attention from its own legal action regarding consumer hardware development.

OpenAI also alleged that Apple monitors former employees through personal accounts after they depart. With legal battles escalating, both firms await preliminary court hearings to decide whether strict restrictions on employee movements between competitors will stand.

Retailer Berli Jucker Shuts Underperforming Outlets to Lift Profit

B1+ · 1 min

Berli Jucker, the commercial operator of the Big C retail chain, will close twelve underperforming hypermarkets by the end of the year to improve operational efficiency. The company had already completed eight closures during the first nine months of 2026 as part of a wider portfolio adjustment.

Chief Executive Aswin Techajareonvikul stated that the strategic closures contributed 220 million baht to company trading profit. BJC also modernized fifteen large stores alongside hundreds of smaller branches, achieving an 8.3 percent increase in customer transactions at refurbished locations.

The retailer expects this selective turnaround will sustain long-term retail earnings across urban markets. In an unrelated retail development, The Mall Group announced that The Mall Korat in Nakhon Ratchasima will cease operations in January after failing to secure a viable lease renewal.

Health Experts Warn of Rising Respiratory Disease and Vaping Risks

B1+ · 1 min

Medical professionals in Thailand are calling for urgent public action to address chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, an irreversible lung illness linked to persistent coughing and breathlessness. The warnings were highlighted during a medical forum hosted by King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital for World COPD Day.

Specialists identified air pollution, secondhand tobacco smoke, and biomass cooking fuel as major causes. Assistant Professor Worawan Sirichana warned that ongoing phlegm production and prolonged illness warrant a formal medical evaluation, rather than being dismissed as typical signs of aging.

Doctors also cautioned that e-cigarette use has climbed to 1.7 million individuals nationwide, creating fresh health hazards for youth. Clinicians stressed that nicotine addiction requires structured clinical intervention, urging citizens to avoid viewing vaping as a harmless alternative to conventional smoking.

Feature · Cultures / Digital Life

Designing Healthy Habits for a Clutter-Free Digital Life

B2 · 2 min

Modern working professionals routinely manage hundreds of notifications, group chats, and cloud files daily. While smartphones and online platforms simplify remote collaboration, constant connectivity often leads to mental exhaustion. Digital environments collect clutter just like physical workspaces, and unmanaged digital noise can slowly undermine daily concentration.

The primary danger of digital overload is continuous task switching. Whenever an alert interrupts an ongoing project, the brain expends valuable energy adjusting its attention. Over several hours, this pattern depletes cognitive stamina and diminishes overall job performance. Recognizing this reality enables individuals to view device boundaries not as personal weakness, but as an essential professional requirement.

Developing a sustainable workflow begins with intentional boundary setting. Rather than leaving notification banners active across all devices, workers can schedule designated windows to review non-urgent correspondence. Silencing ambient alerts during deep focus sessions prevents impulsive phone checking. Furthermore, categorizing shared folders and archiving outdated documents at the end of every week minimizes daily visual friction.

Creating device-free zones at home also provides genuine mental recovery. Leaving personal laptops outside the bedroom and replacing bedtime screen time with offline reading encourages better sleep hygiene. Simple friction, such as moving entertainment applications into hidden subfolders, naturally reduces mindless screen habits.

Ultimately, managing personal technology is an ongoing practice rather than a one-time purge. By mindfully structuring notifications and protecting offline hours, professionals can harness technological tools effectively without allowing continuous connectivity to dominate their personal lives.

Real English · Two Takes

Should office professionals completely separate their personal and work devices?

C1 · 2 min

Clear Boundaries Preserve Focus

Carrying separate hardware establishes a vital barrier between private life and office responsibilities. Admittedly, managing two distinct devices can feel slightly inconvenient during daily commutes. Nonetheless, this practical division prevents accidental leaks of sensitive business information and shields employees from after-hours interruptions. In the long run, setting explicit technical boundaries ensures that personal privacy remains completely untangled from corporate oversight.

A Single Device Promotes Simplicity

Relying on a single personal smartphone for occasional office tasks makes daily life significantly smoother. While traditional managers might frown upon mixing personal and work accounts, modern software allows users to isolate corporate profiles securely on one handset. Instead of burdening professionals with extra hardware and cables, this integrated approach keeps digital communication lean, adaptable, and genuinely realistic for modern hybrid schedules.

Thai Speaker's Trap

Word order & questions

Word Order in Indirect Questions

Can you tell me where is your new office?

Can you tell me where your new office is?

Thai speakers often keep question word order inside embedded clauses because Thai questions follow a direct structure like 'อยู่ที่ไหน' (yu thi nai). In English, when a question begins with an introductory phrase like 'Can you tell me' or 'Do you know', the sub-clause shifts to normal statement order (subject + verb).
