Cover Story

Indian National Jailed for Over 19 Years in New York Plot Targeting Sikh Leader

C1 · 3 min

A federal court in Manhattan has sentenced an Indian citizen to 235 months in prison for his role in an attempted murder-for-hire plot. Nikhil Gupta, 55, faced sentencing on Friday after pleading guilty in February to three conspiracy charges, including murder-for-hire and conspiracy to commit money laundering. US District Judge Victor Marrero handed down the term of more than 19 and a half years, noting that Gupta will likely face deportation to India after completing his sentence.

The intended target of the scheme was Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a prominent New York-based Sikh separatist leader and dual US-Canadian citizen. Pannun acts as a legal advocate for Sikhs for Justice, a US-based organisation that campaigns for Punjab to establish an independent sovereign state called Khalistan. India banned the separatist group in 2019 and has accused Pannun of being a terrorist, an accusation that he firmly denies. The Khalistan independence movement has faced steadfast opposition from all political parties in New Delhi.

According to court documents, Gupta was recruited in May 2023 by Vikash Yadav, an Indian intelligence officer, to arrange Pannun's assassination. Yadav allegedly provided Gupta with sensitive details, including Pannun's New York residential address and daily schedule. Acting under Yadav's direction, Gupta reached out to a contact in the United States to arrange the contract killing. That individual, however, was secretly an informant for the US government. The informant introduced Gupta to an alleged hitman, who was actually an undercover officer with the Drug Enforcement Administration.

Investigators revealed that Gupta and Yadav arranged for a cash delivery of $15,000 to the supposed hitman to execute the operation. Authorities subsequently arrested Gupta in Prague, and he remained held without bail following his extradition to the United States in June 2023. While federal prosecutors with the Manhattan US Attorney's Office had sought a sentence of roughly 24 years, Gupta had agreed in a plea deal to serve at least two decades. Yadav has also been charged in the indictment, though he is not currently in American custody.

During Friday's hearing, Gupta expressed remorse, admitting that he regretted straying from the nonviolent principles of Mahatma Gandhi and offering apologies to Pannun, his family, and affected communities. Pannun attended the sentencing, describing Gupta as merely a foot soldier operating on behalf of Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi's administration. He stated that the plot was meant to silence his visible campaign, vowing to continue pursuing self-determination for Punjab.

The case has brought bilateral ties under severe diplomatic scrutiny, as multiple alleged plots against Sikh activists have emerged across North America. New Delhi has officially denied any involvement in the assassination plot, maintaining that such foreign operations violate established state policy.

World in Brief

Judge Approves Landmark $7.25 Billion Settlement Over Roundup Weedkiller

B1+ · 1 min

A federal judge in Missouri has granted final approval to a $7.25 billion settlement between German chemical group Bayer and tens of thousands of plaintiffs. The legal action claimed that exposure to Roundup weedkiller caused non-Hodgkin lymphoma, a form of blood cancer.

Judge Timothy Boyer ruled the agreement was fair and adequate. Bayer will pay into a dedicated fund over 21 years to compensate existing and future patients. Although some plaintiff attorneys criticized individual awards of $10,000 to $165,000 as inadequate, the judge noted further delays would harm sick victims.

Roundup's active chemical, glyphosate, remains widely used globally. Bayer inherited the lawsuits after acquiring Monsanto in 2018. While this approval brings substantial relief, about 200 cases still proceed in other federal courts.

New York Mayor Unveils Plan to Shutter Rikers Island Complex by 2032

B1+ · 1 min

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced a comprehensive strategy on Friday to close the notorious Rikers Island jail complex by 2032. The city plans to replace the facility with four modern borough-based jails.

The dilapidated 413-acre complex has long faced criticism over crumbling buildings, severe staffing shortages, and frequent inmate deaths. Under the new proposal, officials intend to reduce the current inmate population from over 6,500 down to 4,100 detainees.

However, the correction officers' union strongly criticized the population limit, warning that arbitrary caps will end up jeopardising public safety. The mayor's administration insisted that safe alternatives exist and that modern detention facilities remain an inevitable requirement for the city.

Thailand Today

Vietjet Thailand Expands Japan Capacity Beyond One Million Seats

B1+ · 1 min

Vietjet Thailand has announced ambitious expansion plans, aiming to provide more than one million seats on routes to Japan next year. The low-cost carrier is adding services to regional destinations such as Fukuoka, Okinawa, and Sapporo.

Thai tourists now represent the largest visitor group from Southeast Asia travelling to Japan. According to the Japan National Tourism Organization, about 70 percent of Thai visitors are repeat travellers who increasingly explore mountain landscapes and nature spots rather than major metropolitan hubs like Tokyo.

Despite surging jet fuel prices, Vietjet reported average passenger load factors near 90 percent. The carrier plans to transition entirely to a fleet of 36 Boeing 737-8 aircraft next year to support further route additions.

Opposition Democrat Party Criticizes Severe Solar Subsidy Scheme Delays

B1+ · 1 min

Thailand's opposition Democrat Party has demanded an explanation from the government after revealing that none of the 70,000 applicants in the national household solar scheme have received payments. The project offers cash incentives of up to 50,000 baht per household.

Democrat economic representative Teerat Ratanaseraporn warned that despite significant budget allocations, the public has not experienced any tangible results. Less than ten months remain before the August 2027 grid connection deadline, yet zero systems have secured formal grid approval.

The party raised concerns that rushed inspections near the programme deadline could compromise safety standards. Officials were urged to perform a clear technical assessment and simplify restrictive agency requirements to help homeowners complete the installation process on time.

Feature · Cultures / Digital Life

The Silent Shift: Rethinking Our Always-On Culture in a Hyper-Connected Era

B2 · 2 min

Modern working life often feels like an unending stream of push notifications, message banners, and calendar reminders. Over the past decade, digital communication tools have seamlessly integrated into our personal routines, quietly erasing the boundary between public engagement and private restoration. Many working adults now operate under an unspoken expectation of immediate responsiveness, checking group chats over dinner and monitoring workplace updates during weekend leisure.

This persistent digital connectivity fundamentally alters how cultural communities interact and build relationships. When our attention remains perpetually divided, shared physical spaces often lose their communal warmth. Meals with family or casual gatherings with peers frequently turn into parallel screen-viewing sessions. While online tools allow us to coordinate logistics instantly and maintain distant friendships, they can also weaken our capacity for patient, uninterrupted listening during face-to-face interactions.

Furthermore, constant connectivity creates subtle psychological exhaustion. The urge to monitor feeds often stems from a fear that we will fall behind on critical developments or professional opportunities. Nevertheless, continuous digital exposure forces the brain to remain in a low-grade state of vigilance. Without quiet intervals for cognitive processing, creativity declines, and daily problem-solving becomes reactive rather than deliberate.

Reclaiming autonomy in this connected culture does not require abandoning modern devices entirely. Instead, individuals can establish predictable digital boundaries that honour personal wellbeing. Simple habits, such as designating phone-free zones during family meals or silencing non-urgent notifications after evening hours, help restore mental clarity. Teams can also agree on sensible response windows rather than expecting instant replies outside regular business hours.

Ultimately, technological devices should support human connection rather than dominate daily existence. By exercising conscious intention over when and how we engage with digital platforms, we protect our mental space, enrich our real-world relationships, and build a sustainable cultural balance in an increasingly automated world.

Real English · Two Takes

Should urban professionals practice a strict digital detox by disconnecting from personal social media every weekend?

C1 · 2 min

A Vital Reset for Focus

Taking two days away from algorithmic feeds restores mental clarity and protects personal downtime. Although it is true that modern social platforms keep us connected with friends, continuous scrolling drains cognitive energy and fuels unhealthy social comparisons. Disconnecting over the weekend allows working adults to engage deeply in offline hobbies, spend quality time with loved ones, and experience genuine rest without the constant itch to check notifications. Stepping away temporarily breaks compulsive digital habits and builds emotional resilience for the workweek ahead.

An Unrealistic Routine

It could be argued that complete disengagement creates unnecessary friction in our daily lives. Modern social platforms are not merely entertainment channels; they are practical tools used to coordinate weekend meetups, discover local cultural events, and keep in touch with distant relatives. On the other hand, imposing an all-or-nothing ban often produces anxiety regarding missed messages or urgent updates. Practicing moderate, mindful usage throughout the day is far more sustainable than enforcing rigid, artificial blackouts that disrupt real-world weekend planning.

Thai Speaker's Trap

Word order & questions

Question Word Order: Putting Auxiliary Verbs in the Right Place

Why you did not reply to my message this morning?

Why didn't you reply to my message this morning?

Thai speakers often transfer Thai sentence structure by placing question words like 'why' (ทำไม) directly before the subject and verb without moving the auxiliary verb. In standard English questions, the auxiliary verb ('did', 'do', or 'is') must appear before the subject: 'Why didn't you...' rather than 'Why you did not...'.

Word Root

tourner (Latin: tornare) = to turn or spin on a lathe

In English tour, tournament, contour

French travelers used the root when describing moving around a circuit, which later entered English as tourism.

Borrowed into English

Curious Word

Error Identification

  1. The government is offering generous incentives to encourage families to adopt solar technology. — incentive takes a to-infinitive, not for + base verb
  2. If employees do not update their digital skills regularly, they will fall behind their peers. — fall behind does not take the preposition of
  3. Frequent project delays are seriously jeopardising the municipality's clean energy targets. — jeopardise is a transitive verb and takes no preposition
  4. Under intense public scrutiny, the aviation authority conducted a thorough assessment of the runway safety protocols. — assessment collocates with the preposition of when denoting the target of appraisal
  5. Without sustained investment, higher ticket prices will become an inevitable result of expanding the fleet. — result takes the preposition of to express source or cause
