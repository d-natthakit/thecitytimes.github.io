Cover Story

Philippines’ Carmelle Claims Victory in First Asian Eurovision Edition

C1 · 3 min

Nineteen-year-old Carmelle Collado from the Philippines made history in Bangkok on Saturday night, winning the inaugural Eurovision Song Contest Asia. Performing under the single name Carmelle, she took top honors at the One Bangkok Forum with her entry, Sobra-Sobra. The triumph marks a landmark moment for the franchise, being the first regional edition staged outside Europe. Carmelle ended with 211 points, leaving host country Thailand in second place with 170 points and Vietnam in third with 164 points.

Carmelle’s winning song mixes modern pop and energetic rap with traditional Philippine instruments, namely the kubing and kulintang. Sung in Tagalog and English, the anthem celebrates female strength and independent women. Growing up near railway tracks in the Bicol region of Luzon, Carmelle has performed in televised talent competitions since age twelve. Following her win, she dedicated her success as a tribute to all Filipino women, expressing gratitude to fans worldwide who supported her on this global stage.

The event hosted eleven competing nations in a vibrant two-and-a-half-hour show. Opening performances celebrated Thai culture, featuring Muay Thai fighters alongside drag artists on street food carts before contestants arrived in tuk-tuks. The varied lineup included a cappella acts, rock ballads, and traditional attire. Bangladesh’s Beatmosphere performed without instruments, while Bhutan’s Nyendra wore a traditional gho against a Himalayan backdrop. Nepal’s singer Pratik Himal Rai delivered a rock performance while appealing for international support following recent devastating floods near the Chinese border.

The final outcome was determined by combining two separate voting segments. National juries evaluated acts during a Friday rehearsal, awarding the Philippines an initial lead of 93 points. Online public voting provided the remaining half of the total scores, allowing global viewers to submit free votes or purchase additional ballots. Laos representative Thinla suffered technical audio issues during her live set, prompting organizers to broadcast her dress rehearsal video later. Despite the problem, she secured fifth place overall with strong public backing.

Carmelle received a trophy presented by Bulgarian singer Dara, the recent European contest winner, along with an invitation to perform at Eurovision 2027 in Burgas. Organizers emphasized that hosting rights for future editions will not automatically transfer to winning nations due to infrastructure demands in smaller countries. Executive producer Assaf Blecher revealed that sixteen or seventeen territories have already expressed interest in joining next year's event, setting up a resilient platform for expanding the contest across Asia.

World in Brief

Italy Hits Meta with Record Fine Over Photo Privacy

B1+ · 2 min

Italian regulators have fined social media giant Meta 805 million euros for accessing Android users' device photos without explicit consent. The Data Protection Authority stated that Meta failed to inform approximately 20 million users clearly about photo collection inside the Facebook app. Officials gave Meta 60 days to turn off the photo feature by default and ask users for separate permission.

The penalty represents one of the largest financial sanctions ever imposed on a tech firm by Italian authorities. Regulators highlighted the massive number of affected consumers, ongoing violations, and a lack of corporate cooperation. The action targets a feature that scans smartphone galleries to suggest potential media posts. Italian officials noted that initial app notices failed to clarify how photo data would be processed.

Meta strongly disputed the regulatory findings and confirmed plans to appeal the decision. A spokesperson argued that users must grant permission on their devices before the suggestion system operates and can alter settings anytime. The Italian fine follows a separate US court ruling that ordered Meta to pay major damages over data privacy disclosures, increasing regulatory scrutiny on its global practices.

Hungarian Probe Recovers Hidden Cash in Corruption Case

B1+ · 2 min

Hungarian authorities have recovered nearly 20 million dollars in hidden cash during an official corruption investigation involving a former government minister. The newly formed National Asset Recovery and Protection Office discovered the funds during a two-day search in Budapest and three surrounding counties. Officials found banknotes buried in forests, hidden in farm buildings, and locked in a remote safe.

The seized money is linked to Miklos Sesztak, who served as national development minister under former Prime Minister Viktor Orban between 2014 and 2018. Sesztak was arrested in late September under suspicion of taking over 37 million dollars in bribes connected to state bus purchases and construction contracts. He has denied all criminal charges through his legal representation.

Current Prime Minister Peter Magyar, set to spearhead anti-graft initiatives, praised the cash recovery as a turning point in his administration's reform efforts. Magyar's party won a decisive victory in April, promising to overhaul oversight and improve international relations. Authorities confirmed that all recovered funds have been deposited into state treasury accounts while broader financial investigations continue.

Thai Executives Warn of Rising Economic Pressures

B1+ · 2 min

Thai business leaders are advising companies to prepare for significant economic and technological challenges over the next three years. Speaking at a sustainability panel in Bangkok, top executives from Siam Cement Group and Frasers Property Thailand warned that firms failing to prepare risk severe financial damage. They emphasized that any operational disruption could harm customer trust and supply chains.

Industry leaders noted that local firms face intense competition from imported goods and industrial overcapacity in mainland China. To remain competitive, companies must manage borrowing costs, monitor interest rates, and improve energy efficiency. Executives highlighted established corporate codes of conduct as useful tools for setting shared expectations with suppliers, while encouraging business leaders to outperform market expectations through sustainable practices.

The panel also discussed how rapid automation, artificial intelligence, and advanced robotics will impact employment. Speakers called for updated educational programs to train workers for high-value tasks alongside modern technology. Additionally, executives urged policymakers to refine property policies to attract foreign investment and retain top international talent in competitive sectors like semiconductor manufacturing.

Cambodian Opposition Figure Denounces Phnom Penh Summit

B1+ · 2 min

Exiled Cambodian opposition leader Sam Rainsy has criticized an international summit taking place in Phnom Penh this weekend, calling the gathering improper. Leaders and senior ministers from French-speaking nations gathered for the International Organisation of La Francophonie summit. Rainsy argued from Paris that ongoing political persecution in Cambodia conflicts directly with the organization's stated values of democracy and human rights.

Human rights groups and international representatives have voiced similar concerns regarding the host nation. A United Nations envoy urged the Cambodian government to release over 100 detained activists before the event opened. Meanwhile, local civic groups submitted petitions highlighting democratic decline and civil society restrictions. French officials acknowledged these concerns, stating that human rights issues are being addressed through direct diplomatic dialogue rather than boycotts.

Cambodian officials dismissed the criticisms, stating the government is focused on running a successful international event. Political observers note that government pressure on independent media and political opposition remains strong following decades of single-party dominance. The nation also faces growing international pressure to rectify illegal online fraud operations operating within its borders, which continue to draw global concern.

Mastering Effective Service Delivery and Customer Communication

B2 · 2 min

In today's fast-paced commercial landscape, maintaining strong customer relationships requires clear communication and structured service management. Whether handling daily retail transactions, administrative requests, or complex corporate accounts, front-line professionals must manage client expectations efficiently. Every business transaction undergoes moments of friction, making transparent interaction vital for long-term customer loyalty.

To deliver high-quality service, organizations must establish standard communication procedures. When unexpected delays arise, acknowledging the issue early prevents customer frustration. Front-line staff should communicate current project statuses clearly without relying on dense jargon. Presenting direct solutions alongside clear timelines helps unveil an organization's commitment to quality while reassuring clients during critical project updates.

Handling demanding client scenarios also requires flexible negotiation skills. When facing unreasonable demands or policy constraints, taking a proactive stance helps staff offer viable alternatives rather than simple refusals. Explaining organizational guidelines calmly helps customers understand boundary limitations while keeping conversations productive and polite.

Effective service delivery relies on deep domain expertise and consistent follow-up. Teams that monitor service inquiries systematically can identify recurring operational bottlenecks before they escalate. By analyzing customer feedback continuously, businesses can refine internal workflows, resolve service complaints smoothly, and protect critical revenue streams in an increasingly competitive marketplace.

Handling a Customer's Urgent Order Change

B2 · 2 min

A customer contacts your service team asking to modify a confirmed order right before dispatch. You need to explain that while the package is already packed, you will try to update it.

RegisterHow to say it
FormalWe acknowledge your request to modify the order. Although processing is nearly complete, our team will collaborate with dispatch to attempt an adjustment.
NeutralWe have received your change request. The package is already prepared for shipping, but we will check if our team can update the items for you.
CasualGot your message about changing the order! It is already packed up, but let me quick check if we can fix it for you before it goes out.

Useful Phrases

  • We have received your request regarding...
  • Although processing is nearly complete...

Thai Speaker's Trap

Confusing word pairs (e.g. bored/boring)

Confusing 'Bored' and 'Boring'

The customer was very boring because the service was slow.

The customer was very bored because the service was slow.

In English, adjectives ending in '-ed' describe how a person feels, while adjectives ending in '-ing' describe the thing or person that causes the feeling. Saying 'the customer was boring' means the customer was not interesting, which translates incorrectly from the Thai idea of 'ลูกค้ารู้สึกเบื่อ' (the customer felt bored).
