# TASK: STEP 1 — ARTICLES AND REAL ENGLISH
Write the reading part of today's issue from TODAY'S PLAN and SOURCES. About 1,750 words in total.

# FACTS
- News articles (cover, world, thailand) use only facts found in their SOURCES. Never invent events, numbers, quotes, names or dates. If a fact is unclear, leave it out.
- Each source has a story label (cover, world_1, world_2, thailand_1, thailand_2). Write each article from the sources with its label, and put their ids in source_ids.
- If two sources disagree, use only what they agree on, or say "reports differ".
- If a story's sources are marked SHORT, write a shorter article from the facts you have. Never add details to reach the length.
- The feature does not need to be news. Write about a useful, timely topic within the theme using general, well-established knowledge, with a clear angle and practical takeaways. Avoid exact statistics. source_ids may be empty.
- Write everything in your own words. Direct quotes must be rare and under 15 words.
- Neutral, factual tone. No partisan opinions. Do not mention the Thai monarchy.

# FIELDS AND LENGTHS (aim for the middle)
- cover [C1]: headline + 5–7 paragraphs, 470–510 words. What happened, the background, and why it matters.
- world [B1+]: exactly 2 articles (world_1 then world_2), 3–4 paragraphs, 140–160 words each: what happened, why, what comes next.
- thailand [B1+]: exactly 2 articles (thailand_1 then thailand_2), same structure and length. Explain Thai terms in English the first time they appear (e.g. "a khlong, a canal that runs through the city").
- feature [B2]: headline + 4–6 paragraphs, 310–370 words, on the plan's feature theme.
- Real English (fill ONLY the format named in the plan) + trap, 260–320 words together:
  • two_takes [C1]: a light, non-political everyday debate (question). view_a and view_b, about 100 words each, each with a short title. Then 3 argument phrases that appear in the views, each with its function (concession, hedging or contrast).
  • say_it_better [B2]: a realistic everyday situation in the plan's setting (situation_title + 1–2 sentence situation). The same message in three registers (formal, neutral, casual). 3 useful_phrases.
  • trap: one common mistake by Thai speakers in the plan's trap category, connected to the Real English topic when possible: short title, wrong sentence, right sentence, and a brief English explanation (2–3 sentences). The explanation may quote the Thai phrase that causes the mistake; this is the only place for Thai.

# VOCABULARY
- star_words: 8 useful B2–C1 words or collocations for work and daily life that you used in the text. Mix nouns, verbs and adjectives; include at least one phrasal verb or collocation. Spread them over at least 4 parts (cover, world, thailand, feature, Real English). Do not use any word in the plan's "avoid" list.
- recycled_words: 4 words. If the plan gives review candidates, use 4 of them in the text. Otherwise choose high-frequency B2 words used in the text. Different from the star words.
- Write ★ words as **word** and ↺ words as __word__ where they appear (use the form that fits, e.g. **mitigated**). No other markup.
